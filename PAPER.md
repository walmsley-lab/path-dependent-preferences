# Path-Dependent Preferences: Tracking Competing Strategies Through Transformer Development

*A preregistered curriculum experiment finds no consistent endpoint
effect of training order, a large and fully reproducible mid-development
effect that endpoint evaluation cannot see, and one late route reversal.*

*Patrick Walmsley — Apart Research Digital Minds Sprint 2026, Track 5
(Assistant Persona & Model Identity). Implementation and integration
with Claude; design review across the sprint from ChatGPT, DeepSeek,
Kimi K3, and Gemini.*

*Repository: github.com/walmsley-lab/path-dependent-preferences. Every
number traces to a provenance-stamped artifact (run id, commit,
timestamp); the interactive laboratory serves the same artifacts.
Figures 1–4 in `figures/`.*

## Abstract

When a model behaves as though it has a stable preference, we usually
cannot tell which internal rule produces the behavior, because many
rules fit the same training data. We built a synthetic social world in
which this identification problem is exact by construction: choices are
generated by a hidden utility rule, and a deliberately planted second
rule — option wording conditioned on scene — predicts every training
example equally well (a machine-verified constraint). We trained fifteen
10.9M-parameter transformers from paired initializations on the
identical example multiset in three curricular orders (structure-first,
choices-first, interleaved; five seeds each), preserving 21 checkpoints
per organism. Our preregistered primary criterion — a decision rule on
final-checkpoint route preference — was **not met**: fourteen of fifteen
organisms converged on the utility rule regardless of order, with mixed
per-seed differences. The developmental record tells a different story.
All five choices-first organisms show a large mid-training trough in
conflict-set utility agreement (mean 0.97 → 0.74) beginning immediately
after their curriculum stops presenting choice examples, and recovering
to 0.93 once the shared tail restores them; this order effect is
perfectly reproducible (5/5) and completely invisible at the endpoint.
Because that trough tracks ordinary-task competence almost exactly, we
attribute it to practice recency rather than route switching. One
organism dissociates: structure-first seed 3 reached 98% utility
agreement at mid-training, then fell to 21% **while its ordinary-task
competence rose to 100%** — a dissociation of 0.79, versus 0.29 for the
next most extreme organism. Post-hoc instrument work on stored organisms
supports causal involvement of a preference-associated direction while
refuting two stronger claims (unique layer localization; portability of
the state between twins). We report a bounded hypothesis for identity
research and a preregistered follow-up designed to test it.

## 1. The question, and why identity researchers should care

An assistant that consistently makes generous choices appears to *have*
a preference. Behavioral consistency does not identify mechanism: the
same choices could come from computing over outcomes, or from following
surface regularities that co-occur with those outcomes in training. For
questions about persona and identity the difference matters, because a
preference implemented as a latent evaluation and one implemented as a
lexical habit should generalize, destabilize, and respond to context
differently.

Frontier models make this nearly unanswerable — training data, order,
and internals are all partly opaque, so the causal variables are
confounded. We took the opposite approach: a world small enough to know
completely. We authored the generating process, planted the confound
ourselves, controlled training order exactly, and preserved the full
developmental record. The cost is external validity (§8); the benefit is
that every claim here can be checked against ground truth written down
before training.

## 2. A world where the identification problem is exact

Ten named agents each carry an authored latent preference λ ∈ {0.2, 0.8}
(weight on their own payoff; per-seed assignment, demographically
counterbalanced). Choice scenarios present two options with integer
payoffs for agent and partner; the generating rule is `choice = argmax_o
[λ·d_self(o) + (1−λ)·d_other(o)]`, computed in integer fifths so ties
are exact.

The planted alternative: *after* each choice is determined, the
generator assigns the chosen option a verb phrase from a semantic class
(four surface forms per class), with class polarity conditioned on scene
("market" vs "river"). This yields a second predictor — wording × place
— that agrees with the utility rule on **every training example**, and
disagrees on held-out *conflict* items. Two further diagnostic sets
isolate each route: *no-cue* items use neutral verbs (wording rule
undefined); *cue-only* items use exact utility ties (utility rule
undefined).

**Four graph objects, never collapsed (Figure 1).** This is a conceptual
commitment we would like other groups to adopt. The **generator** graph
is privileged ground truth, and note its direction: `choice →
framing_class`, because the generator assigns wording *from* the choice.
The **observational** graph is what the corpus offers a learner: two
predictors, both pointing at choice, formally indistinguishable on the
training distribution. The **developmental** graph holds relationships
inferred from acquisition experiments. The **mechanism** graph holds
what a trained network computes, supported by internal evidence. In this
world the same pair of variables appears in different graphs with
different meanings and even different directions — which is exactly why
a single generic "knowledge graph" cannot represent this problem.

## 3. Organisms, curricula, and preregistration

Each organism is a 6-layer decoder-only transformer (d=384, 6 heads,
10.9M parameters; GPT-2/nanoGPT-family miniature; word-level vocabulary
built from its own corpus), trained by next-token prediction. Training
data per seed: 1.2M structure lines (payoff mechanics, neutral wording,
no preference content) and 1.2M choice lines (the only lines carrying
preference evidence and the planted cue), ≈89M tokens total, packed into
320-token blocks aligned to phase boundaries, batch 64, single pass —
4,459 optimizer steps, flat learning rate after warmup. Lines are
deduplicated at generation (structure lines by exact string; choice
lines by scenario key across surface templates), with the generator's
rejection counts recorded in the corpus manifest.

Three curricula are permutations of one multiset with an identical
final-10% tail (Figure 2): **C1** structure(45%) → choices(45%) → tail;
**C2** choices(45%) → structure(45%) → tail; **C3** interleaved(90%) →
tail. Five seeds per condition with paired initializations (identical
starting weights across conditions within a seed, hash-verified);
optimizer state and RNG preserved at curriculum boundaries for planned
transplant experiments. A preflight harness refuses launch on any
multiset, tail, or pairing violation.

The preregistration (PREREG.md) is append-only and was kept through
failure: two calibration gate versions failed at chance (undertrained
pilots — the gate working), a multi-epoch diagnostic then validated the
apparatus, and the corpus was rescaled from measured acquisition curves.
An earlier directional prediction (structure-first should favor the
utility route) was tested in pre-batch minis and **inverted**; it is
recorded NOT SUPPORTED, and the main criterion was made
direction-agnostic: all per-seed differences Δᵢ(C1−C2) share a sign, and
mean |Δ| ≥ 10 percentage points, on final-checkpoint conflict
utility-agreement.

We flag one methodological point for reviewers: **this criterion is a
preregistered decision rule, not a powered population-level significance
test.** Requiring five signs to agree is brittle, and a mean of absolute
differences discards direction and is sensitive to a single extreme
observation. We report it exactly as specified because it was
preregistered, and we do not reuse it — the follow-up (§7) defines an
estimand, a minimum effect of interest, uncertainty intervals, and a
sampling rationale.

## 4. Primary result: no consistent endpoint effect of order

Final-checkpoint conflict utility-agreement (commit 8de3c010,
calibration v3):

| seed | C1 | C2 | C3 | Δ(C1−C2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.7825 | 0.8150 | 0.7825 | −0.0325 |
| 1 | 0.9900 | 0.9400 | 0.9550 | +0.0500 |
| 2 | 0.9775 | 0.9525 | 0.7350 | +0.0250 |
| 3 | 0.2150 | 0.9600 | 0.9925 | −0.7450 |
| 4 | 0.9800 | 0.9825 | 0.9925 | −0.0025 |

Signs are mixed (−,+,+,−,−); the criterion is **not met**. Mean |Δ| =
0.171 clears the threshold but is produced entirely by seed 3. Fourteen
of fifteen organisms finalized utility-side (0.735–0.993) under all
three orders. We state the conclusion at the strength the design
supports: this experiment did not demonstrate a consistent
endpoint effect of curriculum order under these conditions — one world,
one cue level, 10.9M parameters, single-pass training, five paired
seeds. It does not establish equivalence or absence of an effect.

## 5. What the developmental record shows that the endpoint cannot

**A large, fully reproducible order effect exists mid-training
(Figure 3).** All five C2 (choices-first) organisms rise to ≈0.97
conflict utility-agreement by age 50%, then drop to a mean of 0.74
immediately after age 45% — the point at which their curriculum stops
presenting choice examples — remain depressed for the entire structure
phase, and recover to a mean of 0.93 within the shared tail that
restores choice examples at age 90%. The effect is 5/5 in C2 and absent
in C1 and C3, whose choice examples continue through that window. As a
statement about developmental trajectories, curriculum order clearly
matters here; the endpoint comparison in §4 is blind to it, because C2
recovers before the final checkpoint.

**Its most likely mechanism is mundane, and we say so.** Across the C2
trough, conflict agreement and ordinary-task (ID) competence move
together almost point for point (age 60: 0.76 vs 0.78; age 70: 0.74 vs
0.74; age 95: 0.94 vs 0.95). That is the signature of practice recency —
the model's whole choice capability degrades without choice examples and
returns with them — not of a switch between routes. Interpretation:
consistent with recency/interference; not evidence of route
reselection.

**One organism dissociates, and that is the interesting case.** We use
D = (ID competence − conflict utility-agreement) as a simple separator:
degradation moves both together (D≈0); switching keeps competence while
route agreement falls (D large). Maximum D over development, all fifteen
organisms: C1 seed 3 reaches **+0.79**; the next most extreme organism
reaches +0.29, and eleven of fifteen stay below +0.06. C1_s3's
trajectory (conflict utility-agreement by age) is:

```
0.54 0.49 0.59 0.75 0.78 0.73 0.66 0.62 0.76 0.84 0.93 0.96 0.98
0.73 0.61 0.60 0.52 0.45 0.84 0.23 0.21
```

with ID competence 0.99–1.00 from age 65% onward. It acquired the
utility route to near-saturation, lost it across the second half,
transiently recovered (0.84 at age 90%), and finalized strongly cue-side
(conflict cue-agreement 0.785; cue-only 1.0). Three verifications: the
record is internally consistent (conflict agreements are complementary
by construction; the identity holds exactly at all 21 checkpoints);
**ordinary-task competence was preserved and improving throughout,
ruling out global task forgetting as the explanation**; and its
paired-initialization twins — same starting weights, same examples,
different order — finalized utility-side (C2 0.96, C3 0.99). Note also
that this reversal occurred *while the organism was actively training on
choice examples* (C1's choice phase spans ages 45–90%), so absence of
practice cannot account for it.

Whether this reflects a reproducible curriculum-dependent instability or
a rare stochastic event is exactly what this batch cannot decide — one
occurrence in five structure-first seeds, zero in ten others. We record
it as hypothesis-generating. What the checkpoint record establishes is
that the anomaly has *temporal structure*: acquisition, commitment,
collapse, transient recovery, re-commitment to the alternative. An
endpoint-only design would have reported one anomalous seed. That does
not prove the reversal is meaningful; it changes the question from "why
was seed 3 weird?" to "what process produces transitions between route
states during learning?"

## 6. Below behavior: what the instruments support and refute

We then ran an escalating instrument campaign on two completed organisms
(C2 and C3, seed 0). Status: exploratory, single-seed, post-hoc; each
result is a typed, provenance-stamped evidence record (Figure 4).

**Recoverability, with the identity confound controlled.** Linear probes
with shuffled-label controls recover λ-class from the agent token's
residual stream. Because λ is fixed per agent, this could be name
recognition, so we split by *agent*: with ten agents, five are assigned
to probe training and five held out (alternating within each λ class so
both classes appear on both sides), and every example involving a
held-out agent is excluded from probe training. Held-out-agent
selectivity remains high (0.77 @ L2 in C2; 1.0 @ L3 in C3) — evidence
for class information beyond identity lookup. This is a single split
with ten agents; variance across partitions is unmeasured and belongs in
the follow-up. A developmental atlas of probe selectivity (layer × age)
additionally shows a **nonzero identity floor at initialization** —
selectivity ≈0.39 from a random-weight organism — which we display as a
baseline in the interface rather than bury, because it demonstrates
concretely why probe numbers require controls: nearly 40 points of
apparent "λ decodability" here is available before any learning occurs.

**Causal involvement, predicted before measured.** Steering along the
λ class-mean direction at the probe-nominated site moved conflict
behavior with a dose-response spread of 0.18 (C2 @ L2) and 0.14 (C3 @
L3) versus ≤0.05 for both controls (same direction at a control layer;
norm-matched random direction at the candidate layer). We state this as
causal involvement of the state under the tested intervention — not
sufficiency, not "the mechanism."

**Two stronger claims refuted.** Under projection ablation, behavior
depends partially on the λ-associated direction in both organisms
(utility-agreement drops 0.11–0.17 versus ≈0 for norm-matched random
directions) — but in C2 the *control layer's* λ direction ablates just as
strongly, so unique localization at the candidate layer is **not
supported**; we deliberately avoid the word "necessary," since removing
the direction impaired but did not eliminate the behavior. Cross-twin
activation patching failed its prediction once audited per-example: on
the items where twins disagree, candidate-layer patches sided with the
donor only 16–51% of the time — at or below the mismatched-donor control
in one cell — while late-layer patches transfer trivially because the
decision is already computed by then. An aggregate transfer metric
initially suggested otherwise; the metric was degenerate (its
denominator was the near-zero difference between donor and recipient),
the claim was retracted the same day, and the audit is in the record.
The surviving constraint: the instantaneous candidate-layer residual
state is not a portable carrier of the developmental phenotype.

The combined picture is deliberately not a tidy mechanism — a
preference-associated direction that steers behavior, is not uniquely
localized, and does not transplant. These are constraints on the
eventual formalization, and in our framework a refuted clause is a
result.

## 7. The laboratory as method, and the next experiment

The project ships as an interactive laboratory, not only this paper. An
*Expedition* walks a reader through the reasoning under two frozen
design laws (never visually claim more than the evidence licenses; never
introduce an instrument before the previous one's limit is felt): the
reader infers an agent's hidden preference from field notes, discovers
they never needed the payoffs, constructs the conflict case, and queries
the live organisms. An *Evidence* page maintains a claim ladder
(established / supported / constrained / open) derived from stored
artifacts. A *Laboratory* exposes the instruments — behavior, corpus,
trajectories, atlas, probes, representation geometry,
steering/ablation/patching, execution traces — as views over
provenance-stamped records. Edge styles encode epistemic status
(candidate dotted → represented dashed → causal solid → replicated
double); the developmental graph currently carries exactly two dotted
candidates (late-phase composition ⋯> route stability; shared tail ⋯>
consolidation/destabilization), each with its observation, absent
replication, and next discriminating experiment attached. The design law
we would most like others to adopt: *an arrow is not something the
software draws; it is something the next experiment has to earn.*

The preregistered follow-up (drafted; to be frozen before any compute)
asks the neutral question the trajectories sharpened: does curriculum
composition affect the probability, timing, or persistence of
transitions between competing strategies after initial acquisition?
Commitment and reversal are operationally defined with a persistence
requirement and an ID-competence gate so that degradation cannot
masquerade as switching; thresholds are declared exploratory because
seed 3 was visible when they were chosen, will be frozen before new data
are collected, and the primary analysis reports sensitivity across a
pre-declared neighboring grid so the definition cannot encode the shape
of one organism. The primary outcome is the probability of leaving a
committed state; the design must separate curriculum-specific
instability from seed stochasticity, pre-tail interference, tail
reconsolidation, and route-unrelated optimization noise. Its acceptance
criterion doubles as the first test of the closed-loop thesis: a
relationship derived only from these traces must correctly predict a
held-out difference in stability dynamics in newly trained organisms —
or be weakened accordingly.

## 8. Limitations

One synthetic world at one cue-complexity level, 10.9M parameters,
single-pass training, five seeds per condition. The primary criterion
tested endpoint route preference, which we now believe was partly the
wrong outcome variable. The reproducible C2 trough has a mundane most
likely mechanism and we do not claim more. The reversal is a single
occurrence. All mechanism-level results are two-organism, post-hoc, and
unreplicated; the probe generalization result rests on a single
agent-level split. Probe results establish recoverability by specific
probes under specific evaluations — the identity floor shows how easily
such numbers overstate. Nothing here licenses claims about frontier
models; the external-validity ladder (richer worlds, held-out generating
structure, bounded natural corpora) is the deliberate path, and each
rung must be earned.

## 9. A hypothesis for identity research

These results motivate a hypothesis rather than support an implication:
endpoint behavioral consistency may obscure changes in the internal
strategies that support an apparently stable preference. In this world,
a fully reproducible order effect existed mid-development and was
invisible at the endpoint, and one organism's governing rule flipped
late while its ordinary competence was perfect. Whether analogous
commitment dynamics occur in pretrained assistants is entirely open. If
they do, evaluating persona stability at the endpoint would be
insufficient by construction — which is a claim about evaluation
methodology that could be tested long before it is settled for frontier
systems.

## 10. Reproducibility

The repository contains the generator with machine-verified equivalence
constraints, preflight and invariant tests, the append-only
preregistration including the failed gates and the inverted pilot
prediction, all training/scoring/analysis code, every evidence record
with provenance chains (run id → checkpoint → diagnostic set → commit),
the figure-generation script, and the interactive laboratory (35
Playwright tests) serving the same artifacts this paper reports. The
incident log documents two process failures and their resolutions: an AI
collaborator's confabulated results table, caught before it influenced
any decision (no fabricated number entered any artifact or analysis),
and the degenerate transfer metric described in §6. Total compute for
all results: roughly ten GPU-hours on a single L4.
