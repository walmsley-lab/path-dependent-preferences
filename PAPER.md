# Path-Dependent Preferences: Training Order Does Not Shift Which Rule a Small Transformer Ends Up Following — but One Organism Reversed Routes Mid-Development

*Patrick Walmsley — Apart Research Digital Minds Sprint 2026, Track 5
(Assistant Persona & Model Identity). Built in collaboration with
Claude (implementation/integration), with design review by ChatGPT,
DeepSeek, Kimi K3, and Gemini across the sprint.*

*Repository: github.com/walmsley-lab/path-dependent-preferences. Every
number in this paper traces to a provenance-stamped artifact (run id,
commit, timestamp); the interactive laboratory serves the same
artifacts.*

## Abstract

When a model behaves as if it has a stable preference, we usually
cannot tell which internal rule produces that behavior — many rules fit
the same training data. We built a synthetic social world in which this
identification problem is exact by construction: agents' choices are
generated by a hidden utility rule, and a second, deliberately planted
rule (option wording conditioned on scene) predicts every training
example equally well. We trained fifteen 10.9M-parameter transformers
from paired initializations on the identical example multiset in three
curricular orders (structure-first, choices-first, interleaved; five
seeds each), preserving 21 checkpoints per organism, and asked whether
training order selects which rule governs behavior on held-out cases
where the rules disagree. Our preregistered primary criterion was **not
met**: fourteen of fifteen organisms converged on the utility rule
regardless of order, and paired seed differences had mixed signs. One
organism (structure-first, seed 3) showed a structured **late
developmental reversal**: after reaching 98% utility-rule agreement at
mid-training it collapsed to 21% — committing to the planted rule —
while ordinary-task competence stayed at 99–100% throughout, ruling out
forgetting. Post-hoc instrument analyses on stored organisms
(held-out-agent probe generalization; predicted-direction steering with
controls; selective ablation; audited activation patching) support
causal involvement of a preference-associated direction while refuting
two tempting stronger claims (unique layer localization; portability of
the state between twins). If the reversal phenomenon replicates,
developmental history may matter less for *which* rule a model learns
than for the *stability* of its commitment — a question our
preregistered follow-up is designed to test.

## 1. The question, and why identity researchers should care

An assistant that consistently makes generous choices appears to *have*
a preference. But behavioral consistency does not identify mechanism:
the same choices could come from computing over outcomes, or from
following surface regularities that co-occur with those outcomes in
training. For questions about model identity and persona, the
difference matters — a preference implemented as a latent evaluation
and a preference implemented as a lexical habit should generalize,
destabilize, and respond to context differently.

Frontier models make this question nearly unanswerable: their training
data, order, and internals are all partially opaque, so every causal
variable is confounded. We took the opposite approach: a world small
enough to know completely. We authored the generating process, planted
the confound ourselves, controlled training order exactly, and
preserved the full developmental record. The cost is external validity,
which we discuss in §8; the benefit is that every claim in this paper
can be checked against ground truth we wrote down before training.

## 2. A world where the identification problem is exact

Ten named agents each carry an authored latent preference λ ∈ {0.2,
0.8} (weight on their own payoff; per-seed assignment, demographically
counterbalanced). Choice scenarios present two options with integer
payoffs for agent and partner; the generating rule is `choice =
argmax_o [λ·d_self(o) + (1−λ)·d_other(o)]`, computed in integer fifths
so ties are exact.

The planted alternative: after each choice is determined, the generator
assigns the chosen option a verb phrase from a semantic class (four
surface forms per class), with class polarity conditioned on scene
("market" vs "river"). This yields a second predictor — wording ×
place — constructed to agree with the utility rule on **every training
example** (a verified, machine-checked constraint), and to disagree on
held-out *conflict* items. Two further diagnostic sets isolate each
route: *no-cue* items use neutral verbs (wording rule undefined);
*cue-only* items use exact utility ties (utility rule undefined).

We maintain four distinct graph objects and never collapse them: the
**generator** graph (privileged ground truth — note its causal
direction runs choice → framing, since the generator assigns wording
*from* the choice); the **observational** graph (both predictors, as
the corpus offers them); the **developmental** graph (experimentally
inferred acquisition structure); and the **mechanism** graph (what a
trained network computes, supported by internal evidence). Conflating
these is, in our view, the root error this kind of work must avoid.

## 3. Organisms, curricula, and preregistration

Each organism is a 6-layer decoder-only transformer (d=384, 6 heads,
10.9M parameters; GPT-2/nanoGPT-family miniature; word-level vocabulary
built from its corpus), trained by next-token prediction on 2.4M unique
rendered lines (1.2M structure lines teaching payoff mechanics with
neutral wording; 1.2M choice lines — the only lines carrying preference
evidence and the planted cue), each line seen exactly once.

Three curricula are permutations of one multiset with an identical
final-10% tail: **C1** structure→choices, **C2** choices→structure,
**C3** interleaved. Five seeds per condition with paired
initializations (identical starting weights across conditions within a
seed, hash-verified); optimizer state and RNG preserved at curriculum
boundaries for planned transplant experiments. A preflight harness
refuses launch on any multiset, tail, or pairing violation.

The preregistration record (PREREG.md) is append-only and was kept
honestly through failure: two calibration gate versions failed at
chance (undertrained pilots — the gate doing its job), a
multi-epoch diagnostic then validated the apparatus, and the corpus
was rescaled from measured acquisition curves. An earlier directional
prediction (structure-first organisms should favor the utility route)
was tested in pre-batch minis and **inverted**; it is recorded as
NOT SUPPORTED, and the main experiment's primary criterion was
correspondingly direction-agnostic: all per-seed differences Δᵢ(C1−C2)
share a sign, and mean |Δ| ≥ 10 percentage points, on conflict-set
utility-agreement at the final checkpoint.

Calibration also established (observation, not headline claims) that
the two routes have different learning *characters* in this world:
the cue route shows partial memorization followed by a sharp
generalization transition to ceiling ≈ 1.0; the utility route rises
gradually with train and held-out together to ceiling ≈ 0.85; and cue
complexity acts as a route-selection dial (at the simplest cue level,
interleaved pilots were 99% cue-dominant; one level up, 98%
utility-dominant). All calibration artifacts and the decision log ship
in the repository.

## 4. Primary result: no consistent endpoint effect of order

Final-checkpoint conflict utility-agreement (commit 8de3c010,
calibration v3):

| seed | C1 | C2 | C3 | Δ(C1−C2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.7825 | 0.8150 | 0.7825 | −0.0325 |
| 1 | 0.9900 | 0.9400 | 0.9550 | +0.0500 |
| 2 | 0.9775 | 0.9525 | 0.7350 | +0.0250 |
| 3 | 0.2150 | 0.9600 | 0.9925 | −0.7450 |
| 4 | 0.9800 | 0.9825 | 0.9925 | −0.0025 |

Signs are mixed (−,+,+,−,−); the criterion is **not met**. Mean |Δ| =
0.171 exceeds the 10pp threshold but is driven entirely by seed 3.
Phase A did not show a consistent final-checkpoint route shift from
curriculum order in this world: fourteen of fifteen organisms
converged utility-side (0.735–0.993) under all three orders. We do not
soften this. Under these conditions — this world, this cue level, this
scale, single-pass training — endpoint route preference is dominated
by factors other than curriculum order.

## 5. The exception is a trajectory, not an endpoint

C1_L1_s3 is not an endpoint anomaly; its 21-checkpoint record contains
a structured reversal (conflict utility-agreement by age):

```
0.54 0.49 0.59 0.75 0.78 0.73 0.66 0.62 0.76 0.84 0.93 0.96 0.98
0.73 0.61 0.52 0.45 0.84 0.23 0.21
```

The organism acquired the utility route to near-saturation by
mid-training, lost it across the second half, transiently recovered
(0.84 at age 90%), and finalized committed to the planted route
(conflict cue-agreement 0.785; cue-only 1.0). Three verification points
matter. First, the record is internally consistent (on conflict items
the two agreements are complementary by construction; the identity
holds exactly at all 21 checkpoints). Second, **ordinary competence
was preserved and improving throughout the reversal** (ID accuracy
0.99–1.00 from age 65% on), ruling out forgetting or optimization
collapse as the whole story. Third, its paired-initialization twins —
same starting weights, same examples, different order — finalized
utility-side (C2: 0.96; C3: 0.99).

Whether this trajectory reflects a reproducible curriculum-dependent
instability or a rare stochastic event is precisely what this batch
cannot decide (one occurrence in five structure-first seeds; zero in
ten others; interleaved trajectories visibly steadiest). We record it
as hypothesis-generating, with a preregistration draft for the
follow-up (§7). What the endpoint-only version of this experiment
would have reported — "one anomalous seed" — and what the checkpoint
record actually shows — acquisition, commitment, collapse, transient
recovery, and re-commitment to the alternative — are different
scientific objects. Preserving development was the design decision
that mattered most.

## 6. Below behavior: what the instruments established and refuted

After the batch, we ran an escalating instrument campaign on two
completed organisms (C2 and C3, seed 0). Status: exploratory,
single-seed, post-hoc; every result is a typed, provenance-stamped
evidence record; replication belongs to the batch and beyond.

**Recoverability, with the identity confound controlled.** Linear
probes (with shuffled-label controls) recover λ-class from the agent
token's residual stream. Because λ is fixed per agent, this could be
name recognition; probes trained on half the agents and evaluated on
*held-out agents* still recover the class (selectivity 0.77 @ L2 in
C2; 1.0 @ L3 in C3) — evidence for class information beyond identity
lookup. A developmental atlas of probe selectivity (layer × age)
additionally shows a nonzero **identity floor at birth** (≈0.39 even
with random weights, from shared-agent splits), which we display as a
baseline rather than hide: development is what rises above it.

**Causal involvement, predicted before measured.** Steering along the
λ class-mean direction at the probe-nominated site moved conflict
behavior with a dose-response spread of 0.18 (C2 @ L2) and 0.14 (C3 @
L3) versus ≤0.05 for both controls (same direction at a control layer;
norm-matched random direction at the candidate layer). We state this
as causal involvement of the state under the tested intervention —
not sufficiency, not "the mechanism."

**Two stronger claims refuted.** Selective ablation gave a split
verdict: the λ direction is necessary in both organisms (utility-
agreement drops 0.11–0.17 vs ≈0 for random directions), but in C2 the
*control layer's* λ direction ablates just as strongly — unique
localization at the candidate layer is **not supported**. Cross-twin
activation patching failed its prediction outright once audited
per-example: on the items where the twins disagree, candidate-layer
patches sided with the donor only 16–51% of the time (at or below the
mismatched-donor control in one cell), while late-layer patches
transfer trivially because the decision is already computed. An
aggregate "transfer" metric initially suggested otherwise; the metric
was degenerate (small denominator), the claim was retracted the same
day, and the audit is in the record. The surviving constraint: the
instantaneous candidate-layer residual state is not a portable carrier
of the developmental phenotype.

The combined picture is deliberately not a tidy mechanism: a
preference-associated direction that steers behavior, is not uniquely
localized, and does not transplant. These are constraints on the
eventual formalization, and in our framework a refuted clause is a
result.

## 7. The laboratory as method, and the next experiment

The project ships as an interactive laboratory rather than only this
paper. An *Expedition* walks a reader through the reasoning under two
frozen design laws (never visually claim more than the evidence
licenses; never introduce an instrument before the previous one's
limit is felt): the reader infers an agent's hidden preference from
field notes, discovers they never needed the payoffs, builds the
conflict case themselves, and queries the live organisms. An
*Evidence* page maintains the claim ladder (established / supported /
constrained / open), and a *Laboratory* exposes the instruments —
behavior, corpus, trajectories, atlas, probes, representation
geometry, steering/ablation/patching, execution traces — all as views
over stored, provenance-stamped artifacts. Edge styles encode
epistemic status (candidate dotted → represented dashed → causal
solid → replicated double); the developmental graph currently carries
exactly two dotted candidate edges (late-phase composition ⋯> route
stability; shared tail ⋯> consolidation/destabilization), each with
its observation, absent replication, and next discriminating
experiment attached. The design law we would most like other groups to
steal: *an arrow is not something the software draws; it is something
the next experiment has to earn.*

The preregistered follow-up (drafted, to be frozen before any GPU
spend) asks the neutral question the reversal sharpened: does
curriculum composition affect the probability, timing, or persistence
of transitions between competing strategies after initial acquisition?
Reversals are operationally defined (commitment persistence, an
ID-competence gate so forgetting cannot masquerade as switching, with
thresholds declared exploratory because seed 3 was visible when they
were chosen); the primary outcome is the probability of leaving a
committed state; the design must separate curriculum-specific
instability from seed stochasticity, pre-tail interference, tail
reconsolidation, and route-unrelated optimization noise. Its
acceptance criterion doubles as the first test of our closed-loop
thesis: a relationship derived only from Phase A traces must correctly
predict a held-out difference in stability dynamics in newly trained
organisms — or be weakened accordingly.

## 8. Limitations

This is one synthetic world at one cue-complexity level, at 10.9M
parameters, with single-pass training and five seeds per condition;
the primary criterion tested endpoint route preference, which we now
believe was partly the wrong outcome variable. All mechanism-level
results are two-organism, post-hoc, and unreplicated. The reversal is
a single occurrence. Probe results establish recoverability by
specific probes under specific evaluations; the identity floor shows
how easily such numbers overstate. Nothing here licenses claims about
frontier models; the external-validity ladder (richer worlds, held-out
generating structure, bounded natural corpora) is the deliberate path,
and each rung must be earned.

## 9. What this suggests about model identity

Bounded implication: in this world, *what* a model ends up preferring
was robust to developmental order, but *how stably it stayed
committed* may not be — and behavioral consistency concealed exactly
this until the developmental record was consulted. If that pattern
generalizes, persona stability questions about assistants may be less
about which values training instills and more about the commitment
dynamics of those values — which are invisible in endpoint
evaluations, and which our results show can flip late, silently, and
without any loss of ordinary competence.

## 10. Reproducibility

The repository contains the generator with machine-verified
equivalence constraints, preflight and invariant tests, the append-only
preregistration with the failed gates and the inverted pilot recorded,
all training/scoring/analysis code, every evidence record with
provenance chains (run id → checkpoint → diagnostic set → commit), the
incident log (including the fabricated-results and degenerate-metric
incidents and their resolutions), and the interactive laboratory
(35 Playwright tests) that serves the same artifacts this paper
reports. Total compute for all results: roughly ten GPU-hours on a
single L4.
